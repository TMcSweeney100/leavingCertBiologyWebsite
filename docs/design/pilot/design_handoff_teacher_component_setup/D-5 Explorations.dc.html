Turn 1 · Pack D-5 · teacher component setup · direction 2e Navy · rough

Three directions for the stage-dates editor

Same page, same content, same chrome from D-2 — only the editor differs. They separate on one axis: how much help the page gives with spacing the dates. 1a gives none and stays a form. 1b answers it in numbers. 1c answers it in shape.

Placeholder dates throughout, as supplied. Stage names and completion dates are the real ones. Warning and error states, the phone layouts and the teacher-item flows come after you pick.

My answers to your three questions

Save model — batch. One Save dates for all stages, as the API already works. A teacher sets a year in one sitting; six separate saves is six chances to half-finish, and there is no moment where "nothing was lost" can be confirmed. Teacher items stay one at a time — they are their own endpoints and their own small forms. All three directions show the batch bar.

Checkpoints — show them, read-only, one line. The checkpoint is what the date is for: "Plan discussed with the teacher" tells a teacher what has to happen in class by that date, which is exactly the judgement they are making. It is one 14px grey line per row and never gains a control here. Toggle it off in Tweaks to compare.

Date entry — the native date input, inside the D-1 field row, 16px, with the date echoed in words beneath it: Fri 16 Oct 2026. Native gives the phone picker, keyboard entry and typing for free; a custom popover would be the biggest accessibility risk on the page. The echo is what makes "16 Oct 2026" true — the native control itself renders 16/10/2026 in Ireland — and the weekday is a real check: every placeholder date here is a Friday, so a Sunday would show itself.

1a

The field group, extended

The /teach/classes/new field group with the stage name as the row label. Nothing new is invented, row errors already work, and a teacher's own items nest inside the stage they belong to. The cost: rows are uneven, and the page gives no help at all with spacing — the teacher does the arithmetic.
