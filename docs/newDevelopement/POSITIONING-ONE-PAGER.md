## In two sentences

The Leaving Cert is moving from one written exam per subject to a written exam plus a
major project in nearly every subject. We give students one place to see everything
they're assessed on across all their subjects, and give teachers and school leaders a
clear view of who is on track — without ever touching a student's actual work.

## The problem

Senior Cycle Redevelopment gives every redeveloped subject an Additional Assessment
Component worth at least 40% of the grade, rolling out to 2029. A sixth year now has
several of these running at once, each with its own stages, rules and deadlines, spread
across different subjects and different teachers.

Nobody can see the whole picture.

- **The student** has Biology deadlines in Classroom, Business deadlines in Teams, an
  oral in March, a mock in February and a report deadline in November. There is no one
  place any of that lives.
- **The teacher** finds out who hasn't started by asking thirty people individually, or
  by discovering it at the deadline when it's too late to fix.
- **The school** carries the risk. If a component worth 40% slips, that's a grade
  problem, a parent problem and a Department problem — and the principal signs the form
  saying the process was followed.

## What we do

| For | What they get |
|---|---|
| **Students** | One timeline of every assessed component across every subject. The stages and rules for each project. A dated log of their own work as they go. Help with the mechanical things that cost marks — word limits, referencing, the AI declaration. |
| **Teachers** | One screen showing who is behind, sorted worst first. A way to push a deadline or a piece of revision guidance that lands in the student's planner. |
| **School leaders** | Year-group status by subject. Where a problem is forming, before it's a crisis. No subject knowledge needed to read it. |

## What we deliberately don't do

This matters as much as the feature list, and it's usually the thing that wins the room.

- **We don't store coursework.** Not the reports, not the drafts, not the folios. We hold
  ticks, dates and the student's own notes. If our database leaked tomorrow, nobody's
  project is in it.
- **We're not another Classroom.** We don't host notes, take submissions or do marking.
  Those systems do that well. We're the layer above them — the one that can see across
  subjects, which by design they can't.
- **We're not a notes or grinds site.** We're not competing with StudyClix. Different
  problem entirely: they help you learn the content, we help you get the coursework done.
- **We don't report to parents.** Deliberate. It would change what students record, and
  what they record is the whole point.

## Why now

The first tranche of subjects — Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Business — started in
schools in September 2025, with first exams in 2027. Geography, Engineering, Construction
Technology and PE follow. Everything is redeveloped by 2029.

Right now every school in the country is working this out for the first time, mostly with
spreadsheets and handouts. There is no incumbent, because the problem didn't exist two
years ago.

## Why us

It started inside a real classroom, not a slide deck. The first version was built for one
Biology teacher running the first year of the new Biology in Practice Investigation, and
it is in use.

Everything in the product is built from the actual SEC briefs and NCCA guidelines, not
from a summary — the six stages, the word and image limits, the mark bands, the
submission steps, the AI declaration fields. Teachers notice immediately whether a tool
was made by someone who read the documents.

## What to say in a staffroom

Three lines. Not a pitch — an opener.

> "Every subject's project, oral and practical in one timeline. Not per subject —
> all of them."

> "You'd see who hasn't started without having to ask them."

> "It doesn't hold their work. Just whether they've done it."

## The question we can't answer yet

Whether a school treats this as something they buy, or something a teacher happens to
use. That's the whole business, and it's a conversation with a deputy principal, not a
feature. Pricing follows that answer, not the other way round.

## Notes for whoever writes the copy

- Never say "SEC approved", "SEC compliant", or anything implying endorsement. We are not
  connected to the SEC. Describing the product as *built from* the published briefs and
  guidelines is accurate and is claim enough.
- Never suggest the app submits coursework or forms part of the official submission
  chain. It cannot — the chain is student → class teacher → school authority → Schools
  Portal, with no third-party step. Getting this wrong in copy would be noticed
  immediately by exactly the person we're selling to.
- Don't promise grade improvement. We can't evidence it.
- The log is our strongest single feature. Describe it as a record of the student's own
  process that supports the school's authentication — not as anything the SEC sees,
  because it never does.
- Avoid "AI-powered". The AI feature helps students *declare* their AI use, which is
  nearly the opposite, and the distinction matters to this audience.
- Prices and tiers are not settled. Don't publish any.
